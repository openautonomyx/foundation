# OpenAutonomyX Decision Model

The OpenAutonomyX decision model defines how choices are framed, evaluated, approved, implemented, reviewed, and learned from across humans, agents, systems, policies, products, and operations.

## Purpose

Decisions convert evidence, constraints, objectives, risks, and judgment into governed direction.

Every material decision should be understandable, reviewable, attributable, evidence-backed, and revisable.

## Decision Types

Core decision types include:

- strategic
- tactical
- operational
- policy
- architecture
- security
- privacy
- compliance
- financial
- product
- program
- project
- incident
- access
- trust
- model selection
- automation approval
- exception approval

## Common Decision Fields

Every decision should define:

- identifier
- decision type
- owner
- decision maker
- affected stakeholders
- context
- problem statement
- objectives
- options considered
- constraints
- assumptions
- evidence
- risks
- tradeoffs
- confidence
- recommendation
- approval state
- decision status
- implementation plan where applicable
- review date
- outcome
- rollback or revision path
- audit trail

## Decision Lifecycle

Common decision lifecycle states include:

- proposed
- under review
- approved
- rejected
- implemented
- validated
- superseded
- revoked
- archived

## Evidence

Decision evidence may include:

- source documents
- events
- evaluations
- metrics
- expert review
- policy checks
- user feedback
- incident reports
- benchmarks
- experiments
- risk assessments

Evidence must preserve provenance, confidence, limitations, and review state.

## Human and Agent Decisions

Agents may assist with research, option generation, evaluation, recommendation, and monitoring.

Humans must retain meaningful control over consequential decisions, policy exceptions, risk acceptance, and irreversible actions.

## Decision Records

Material decisions should be recorded as decision records.

Architecture decisions should use ADRs where appropriate. Policy, governance, security, privacy, compliance, and operational decisions should use equivalent structured records.

## Decision Principle

A decision is not just a choice. A decision is a governed commitment made under constraints, supported by evidence, accountable to outcomes, and open to future review.
