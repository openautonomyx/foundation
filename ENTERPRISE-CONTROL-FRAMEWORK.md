# Enterprise Control Framework

The OpenAutonomyX enterprise control framework connects risk, policy, controls, evidence, audit, exceptions, ownership, and review into one governed assurance model.

## Purpose

Enterprise autonomy must be controllable, auditable, and explainable. Controls make governance measurable and reviewable.

## Control Chain

```text
Risk
→ Control Objective
→ Control
→ Policy
→ Procedure
→ Evidence
→ Audit
→ Finding
→ Remediation
→ Review
```

## Control Domains

Core control domains include:

- identity and access
- data protection
- privacy and consent
- security
- reliability
- compliance
- auditability
- traceability
- agent governance
- model risk
- vendor risk
- change management
- incident management
- business continuity
- financial authorization

## Common Control Fields

Every control should define:

- identifier
- control name
- control domain
- risk addressed
- control objective
- policy reference
- owner
- operator
- reviewer
- frequency
- evidence required
- test method
- status
- exceptions
- findings
- remediation plan
- review date

## Evidence

Control evidence should be:

- attributable
- time-stamped
- tamper-evident where practical
- linked to policy
- linked to action or event
- retained according to policy
- reviewable by authorized auditors

## Control Testing

Controls may be tested through:

- automated checks
- manual review
- sampling
- continuous monitoring
- audit procedures
- red-team exercises
- incident review
- policy simulation

## AI and Agent Controls

Agentic systems require controls for:

- identity binding
- tool authorization
- data access
- prompt and instruction governance
- model selection
- output validation
- hallucination risk
- human approval
- escalation
- kill switch or suspension
- evaluation and re-certification

## Compliance Mapping

Controls may map to external frameworks such as SOC 2, ISO 27001, NIST, GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, DORA, and AI governance obligations.

Mappings are informational unless explicitly validated and approved.

## Principle

A control is only useful when it is owned, testable, evidenced, reviewed, and connected to risk.
