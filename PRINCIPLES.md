# OpenAutonomyX Founding Principles

These principles define the constitutional posture of OpenAutonomyX systems, repositories, artifacts, and operating models.

## Foundational Principles

1. Everything is data.
2. Everything is temporal.
3. Everything is governed.
4. Everything is observable.
5. Everything is traceable.
6. Everything is replayable.
7. Everything is composable.
8. Everything interoperates through open protocols.

## State and Time

9. Current state is a projection, not truth.
10. Time is a first-class primitive.
11. Identities persist; relationships evolve.
12. Append over overwrite.
13. History must remain inspectable.
14. Derived state must be reproducible from trusted events where practical.

## Human and System Relationship

15. Humans approve; systems reconcile.
16. Humans must retain meaningful control over consequential outcomes.
17. Every automated action must have an accountable owner.
18. Every recommendation should expose context, assumptions, constraints, and confidence where practical.
19. Override, appeal, rollback, and escalation must be designed as first-class capabilities.

## Architecture Principles

20. AI-native and cloud-native are foundational principles.
21. Governance must be native, not bolted on.
22. Production-grade is the baseline, not the goal.
23. Open standards are preferred over proprietary abstractions.
24. Composability is preferred over lock-in.
25. Sovereignty and portability are default assumptions.
26. Cloud-native must remain cloud-agnostic.
27. Air-gapped and edge-ready operation should be considered by design.
28. Build platforms, not silos.

## Reliability Principles

29. Operational truth must survive vendor changes.
30. External systems are integrations and observation sources, not canonical truth by default.
31. Enterprise systems continuously reconcile toward governed desired state.
32. Prefer declarative systems over imperative orchestration.
33. Prefer proven standards, frameworks, and grounded implementations over ad hoc designs.
34. Every implementation must be secure, maintainable, observable, verifiable, and operationally reliable by default.

## Governance Principles

35. Every artifact must have a purpose, owner, lifecycle state, and review path.
36. Every policy must be testable or explicitly marked advisory.
37. Every exception must be recorded with reason, scope, duration, and approver.
38. Every dependency must be visible and periodically reviewed.
39. Every material decision must be captured as a decision record.
40. Every system must define availability, accessibility, usability, recoverability, and lifecycle expectations.

## Trust Principles

41. Trust is earned, evaluated, and periodically renewed.
42. Identity must be explicit.
43. Access must be justified.
44. Actions must be attributable.
45. Capabilities must be verified.
46. Claims must be evidence-backed.
47. Consent must be respected.
48. Privacy must be protected.

## Improvement Principles

49. Systems should learn, but not silently change their governed behavior.
50. Innovation must pass through evaluation, review, and controlled rollout.
51. Stability and safety take precedence over novelty when they conflict.
52. Continuous improvement must be observable, auditable, and reversible.
