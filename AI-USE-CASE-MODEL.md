# OpenAutonomyX AI Use Case Model

The OpenAutonomyX AI use case model defines how AI and agent use cases are described, qualified, governed, and tracked to value.

## Purpose

A use case is the unit that connects business need to governed AI capability. Defining use cases well is what keeps AI adoption purposeful, safe, and measurable.

## How to Define a Use Case

A well-formed AI use case declares:

- **Identifier and name** — stable reference.
- **Problem and objective** — the need and the desired outcome.
- **Primary actors** — humans, agents, and systems involved.
- **Trigger and context** — what initiates it and under what conditions.
- **Scope** — in-scope and explicitly out-of-scope behavior.
- **Required capabilities** — capabilities, tools, and data scopes needed.
- **Autonomy level** — expected level of agent autonomy.
- **Data and knowledge** — sources, classification, consent, and provenance.
- **Risks** — what could go wrong and the inherent risk rating.
- **Controls and approvals** — governance required to proceed.
- **Success metrics** — value and adoption metrics.
- **Acceptance criteria** — what "working and safe" means.
- **Lifecycle state** — proposed → qualified → piloted → in-production → retired.

## Qualification

Use cases are qualified on value, feasibility, data readiness, risk, and governance fit before build. High-risk use cases require impact assessment and explicit approval.

## Governance Binding

Each use case binds to capabilities, risks, controls, agents, and policies as registry relationships, so value and accountability are traceable end to end.

## Value Realization

Use cases are tracked from objective to outcome to realized value; results feed adoption and reuse decisions.

## Use Case Principle

Define the use case before the agent. Capability without a governed use case is risk without purpose.
