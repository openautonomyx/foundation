# Enterprise Operating Model

The OpenAutonomyX enterprise operating model defines how institutions translate strategy into governed execution, reliable operations, measurable value, and continuous improvement.

## Purpose

Enterprise autonomy must work inside real institutional constraints: accountability, budget, audit, procurement, security, privacy, compliance, operations, change management, risk, support, and measurable business outcomes.

This model connects enterprise strategy, portfolio execution, technology governance, human-agent collaboration, and value realization.

## Enterprise Operating System

OpenAutonomyX treats the enterprise as a governed operating system composed of:

- people
- process
- technology
- data
- policy
- risk
- controls
- evidence
- decisions
- platforms
- integrations
- vendors
- outcomes

## Operating Hierarchy

```text
Mission
└── Strategy
    └── Objective
        └── Key Result
            └── Capability
                └── Portfolio
                    └── Program
                        └── Product
                            └── Project
                                └── Milestone
                                    └── Deliverable
                                        └── Task
                                            └── Action
```

Each level must define owner, sponsor, scope, dependencies, constraints, risks, metrics, funding, governance path, and review cadence.

## Enterprise Stakeholder Model

The operating model recognizes multiple enterprise stakeholders:

- board and executive leadership
- CIO, CTO, CISO, CDO, CFO, COO
- business unit leaders
- product and platform owners
- enterprise architects
- IT administrators
- security operations
- risk and compliance teams
- legal and privacy teams
- finance and procurement teams
- data governance teams
- service management teams
- auditors
- employees and end users
- partners, vendors, and customers

## Enterprise Capability Lifecycle

Capabilities move through a governed lifecycle:

```text
Identify Need
→ Define Capability
→ Assess Risk
→ Approve Investment
→ Design Architecture
→ Procure or Build
→ Integrate
→ Validate Controls
→ Deploy
→ Operate
→ Measure Adoption
→ Realize Value
→ Improve
→ Retire
```

## Human and Agent Collaboration

Enterprise agentic systems must define:

- human owner
- agent purpose
- approved capabilities
- tool access
- data access
- policy boundaries
- approval requirements
- monitoring requirements
- escalation path
- evaluation method
- revocation conditions

Agents assist. Humans remain accountable for consequential decisions, risk acceptance, and institutional obligations.

## Governance Integration

Enterprise execution must integrate with:

- identity and access management
- service management
- change management
- security operations
- data governance
- vendor management
- compliance management
- audit management
- portfolio management
- financial management
- enterprise architecture review

## Value Realization Model

Value realization must be explicit.

Every major capability should define:

- baseline
- target outcome
- business metric
- operational metric
- risk reduction metric
- adoption metric
- owner
- measurement method
- reporting cadence
- review date

## Enterprise Controls

Controls should cover:

- access
- approval
- privacy
- consent
- security
- compliance
- reliability
- observability
- auditability
- traceability
- financial authorization
- vendor risk
- model risk
- agent risk

## Operating Principle

Enterprise autonomy is successful only when it produces trusted outcomes inside governed institutional reality.
