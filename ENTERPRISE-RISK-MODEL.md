# Enterprise Risk Model

The OpenAutonomyX enterprise risk model defines how institutions identify, assess, govern, monitor, mitigate, accept, escalate, and learn from risk in AI-native and agentic operating environments.

## Purpose

Enterprise autonomy introduces new forms of operational, security, privacy, compliance, model, data, vendor, and agent risk.

Risk must be explicit, owned, measured, reviewed, and connected to controls, decisions, exceptions, evidence, and value realization.

## Risk Chain

```text
Risk Source
→ Risk Event
→ Impact
→ Likelihood
→ Inherent Risk
→ Control
→ Residual Risk
→ Decision
→ Treatment
→ Review
```

## Risk Types

Core risk types include:

- strategic risk
- operational risk
- technology risk
- cybersecurity risk
- privacy risk
- compliance risk
- legal risk
- financial risk
- vendor risk
- data risk
- model risk
- agent risk
- automation risk
- reliability risk
- reputational risk
- ethical risk

## Common Risk Fields

Every risk should define:

- identifier
- title
- description
- risk type
- source
- owner
- affected stakeholders
- impacted assets
- likelihood
- impact
- inherent risk rating
- existing controls
- residual risk rating
- treatment plan
- decision owner
- review cadence
- escalation path
- evidence
- status

## Risk Treatment

Risk treatment options include:

- avoid
- reduce
- transfer
- accept
- monitor
- escalate

Risk acceptance must be explicit, approved, time-bounded where practical, and reviewed.

## AI and Agent Risk

AI and agent risk should include:

- hallucination risk
- unsafe action risk
- unauthorized tool use
- data leakage
- prompt or instruction injection
- model drift
- evaluation failure
- over-delegation
- low confidence execution
- lack of explainability
- loss of human control
- trust score degradation

## Risk Evidence

Risk evidence may include:

- audit logs
- events
- evaluations
- incidents
- vulnerability scans
- privacy reviews
- compliance assessments
- model evaluations
- human review
- business impact analysis

## Risk Governance

High and critical risks require defined ownership, treatment, review, and escalation.

Risks connected to legal, privacy, safety, security, or public impact require appropriate human review.

## Principle

Enterprise autonomy is responsible only when risk is visible, owned, controlled, reviewed, and connected to decisions.
