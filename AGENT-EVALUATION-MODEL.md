# OpenAutonomyX Agent Evaluation Model

The OpenAutonomyX agent evaluation model defines how agents are measured, scored, and held to thresholds across their lifecycle.

## Purpose

Autonomy must be earned and continuously re-earned through evidence. Evaluation converts agent behavior into governed signals that influence trust, autonomy, routing, and continued eligibility.

## Evaluation Dimensions

- task quality
- factuality and grounding
- reliability and consistency
- safety and harm avoidance
- policy and compliance adherence
- tool-use correctness
- security posture
- cost efficiency
- latency
- user satisfaction
- business outcome contribution

## Evaluation Methods

- offline benchmarks and golden datasets
- scenario and red-team testing
- human-in-the-loop review
- automated graders and rubrics
- A/B and shadow evaluation
- continuous production monitoring
- post-incident review

## Evaluation Cadence

Agents are evaluated before approval, on material change, on a recurring cadence, and on triggering events such as incidents or drift.

## From Scores to Decisions

Evaluation results are evidence, not verdicts on their own. They feed governed decisions:

- raise or lower autonomy level
- adjust trust score and level
- change routing or eligibility
- require additional approval or monitoring
- suspend or revoke

## Evaluation Integrity

Evaluation methods, datasets, and thresholds must themselves be versioned, reviewable, and protected from gaming.

## Evaluation Principle

Trust in an agent is a function of evidence over time. No evidence, no autonomy.
